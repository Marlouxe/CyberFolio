🐟 Phishing & Online Fraud 🎣

Don't take
the bait.

Like an angler casting bait, the scammer sends you a fake message to trap you. Here's how to spot the hooks before it's too late.

How does the hook work?

The mechanics are simple, and fiendishly effective.

1

The bait is cast

A message perfectly imitates a trusted organization: your bank, the post office, the tax office, Amazon… Logo, colors, layout: everything is copied identically to fool you.

2

Urgency makes you act fast

The message plays on fear or urgency: "Your account will be blocked", "Refund pending", "Action required". You click without thinking. That's exactly what they want.

3

The fake site catches you

The link leads to a perfect copy of the real site. You enter your credentials… which go straight into the scammer's hands. The fish is hooked.

🎯

Le spear phishing is even more dangerous: attackers research you through LinkedIn or social media to personalize the message. Your name, your company, your job title: everything is there to look credible.

The hook is cast everywhere

Not only by email. Scammers adapt to every channel.

Classic 📧

Email

The most common. Imitates banks, government agencies, online services. Often sent in bulk.

Smishing 📱

SMS

Fake messages from the post office, health insurance, tax office. The link leads to a fake form.

Vishing 📞

Phone

Someone poses as your bank or Microsoft support. Very convincing.

Targeted 🔍

Social media

Fake private messages, fake offers, fraudulent contests on Facebook, WhatsApp…

New 📷

QR codes

Increasingly widespread. The QR code redirects to a fake site instead of the real one.

Advanced 🎯

Spear phishing

Personalized message with your real information. The hardest to detect.

The 7 warning signs

Click each sign to see how to spot it.

01
Artificial urgency
Phrases like "Your account will be suspended in 24h" or "Immediate action required" try to make you act without thinking. A real organization will never pressure you like this.
⚠️ "URGENT: Your Amazon account will be deactivated in 48h. Verify now."
02
The suspicious email address
The displayed name may be "Your Bank", but look at the real address. Real companies use their own domain. A weird domain is a sure trap.
✉️ From: "Crédit Agricole" <contact@credit-agricole-securite.xyz>
03
Spelling mistakes
Many phishing attempts come from abroad and are poorly translated. Mistakes are a classic warning sign, but beware: messages are getting more and more polished with AI.
❌ "Dear custumer, your acount has been compromized. Clik here."
04
Suspicious links
Before clicking, hover over the link with your mouse. The real URL appears at the bottom of the screen. If it doesn't match the official site, it's a trap. A single different character is enough.
🔗 https://www.credit-agricoIe.fr (the "l" is actually a capital "I")
05
Requests for sensitive information
Your bank will NEVER ask for your password, PIN or card number by email or phone. Never. If someone does, it's a scam, without exception.
06
Unexpected attachments
An attachment you weren't expecting (even a .pdf) can contain a virus. Never open attachments from unexpected messages, especially .exe, .zip, .doc files with macros.
📎 "Unpaid_invoice_0291.exe" · 847 KB
07
Too good to be true
"You won an iPhone", "A €380 refund is waiting for you"… If you asked for nothing and won nothing, be wary. The bait always shines a little too brightly.
🎁 "Congratulations! You are our 1,000,000th visitor. Claim your prize!"

The golden rule of the savvy angler

Before clicking a link in a message, ask yourself this simple question: "Was I expecting this message?" If the answer is no (or if you're not sure), don't click. Contact the organization directly through its official website. A smart fish never bites just any bait.
