Ransom­ware

Understanding the threat, and knowing how to protect yourself.

⚠ System under control

71:59:59

    Let's see what happens to your files… and what saves them.

    A ransomware is a piece of malicious software that blocks access to your data or your computer, then demands a ransom, usually in cryptocurrency, to unlock them. The word comes from ransom and software.

    In practice, you're faced with a message: your files are encrypted, and you must pay within a set time to get the decryption key. Documents, photos, databases, and sometimes even backups connected to the network: everything can be hit within minutes. Without the key, it's practically impossible to recover your files.

    Encryption

    Every file becomes unreadable, with a key that only the attacker holds.

    Double extortion

    Before encrypting, criminals steal the data and threaten to publish or resell it.

    "RaaS"

    Ransomware-as-a-service: developers rent their tool to affiliates in exchange for a percentage of the ransom.

    An industry: RaaS lets less technical criminals take action, which explains the multiplication of attacks.

    How they get in

    Phishing

    An email with a booby-trapped attachment or link: the number one entry point.

    Weak remote access

    Stolen or weak credentials on a VPN or remote desktop, without two-factor authentication.

    An unpatched flaw

    Software or equipment exposed on the internet that hasn't been updated.

    A service provider

    The attack goes through a partner already connected to your network.

    Often, attackers stay in the network for several days to explore it, steal data and disable defenses. Then they trigger the encryption, preferably on a weekend or a public holiday.

    Who is targeted? Everyone: small businesses, hospitals, local governments, schools, universities, and even individuals. These organizations depend on their data to operate and are often less well protected. ANSSI, the French national cybersecurity agency, ranks ransomware among the main threats to French organizations.
    Should you pay? Authorities, including ANSSI, advise against it: nothing guarantees that the key will be provided or will work, the payment funds criminals, and you can be targeted again.

    My best defense: the 3-2-1 rule

    The backup rule in three numbers. And a backup that has never been tested is a backup you don't know works.

    3copies of your data
    2different media
    1offline, disconnected copy

    Updates

    Install patches quickly: they close the most exploited flaws.

    Two-factor authentication

    Especially on remote access and email: a stolen password is no longer enough.

    Limited rights

    Administrator rights only for those who need them, so an infected machine doesn't contaminate everything.

    Vigilance

    Recognize phishing and know who to report a doubt to. At home: unplugged external drive, official software.

    Your turn: the attack is happening

    Click the actions in the right order. Careful, two of them are traps.

    The right moves: disconnect the machine from the network, alert IT, keep the evidence, file a complaint and notify the CNIL (the French data protection authority) within 72 h if personal data is involved, then restore. The No More Ransom project offers free decryption tools for some ransomware, and cybermalveillance.gouv.fr can support you.

    Very real stories

    • 1989The first

      Joseph Popp mails floppy disks of "AIDS information" to attendees of a WHO conference. They encrypt file names and demand 189 dollars to be sent to a post office box in Panama.

    • May 2017WannaCry

      More than 200,000 computers hit in about a hundred countries. The British health system is paralyzed, Renault is hit too. Researcher Marcus Hutchins stops it almost by accident by registering a domain name: a "kill switch".

    • June 2017NotPetya

      Maersk reinstalls thousands of servers and computers (about 300 million dollars). Saint-Gobain estimates its losses at around 250 million euros.

    • Mai 2021Colonial Pipeline

      The pipeline that supplies a large part of the US East Coast shuts down, after an intrusion through a compromised password on a VPN without two-factor authentication. About 4.4 million dollars paid, partly recovered by the FBI.

    • August 2022In France

      The Corbeil-Essonnes hospital is paralyzed: emergency services disrupted, patients transferred. The hackers demand 10 million dollars before publishing patient data.

    • 2024LockBit

      An international operation takes control of the infrastructure of LockBit, one of the most active groups. Others take over.

    • Autumn 2025Jaguar Land Rover

      The British carmaker's factories are at a standstill for several weeks, along with their suppliers.

    Key takeawayRansomware isn't inevitable. Tested backups, updates, two-factor authentication and a little vigilance prevent most disasters.
