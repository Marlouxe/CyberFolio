// real risks

The real risks, without exaggerating

Let's be clear: not everyone who connects to a public Wi-Fi will get hacked. Attacks require equipment, skills and above all motivation. But some scenarios do exist, and it's better to know them.

01
man-in-the-middle

Passive eavesdropping

On an unsecured network, an attacker can position themselves between you and the network to intercept the data you send. If you visit a site over HTTP (without the "S" of HTTPS), the data travels in clear text and can be read.

✓ Check the padlock in the address bar: HTTPS encrypts your data.
02
Evil Twin

Fake access point

An attacker can create a Wi-Fi network with a misleading name, like "Free_Airport_WiFi" or "Free_Cafe". You connect thinking it's the official network. They see all your traffic.

! Always check the exact network name with a staff member before connecting.
03
malware

Malware injection

In some cases, an attacker can take advantage of the shared connection to try to install malware on your device, especially if your operating system is out of date.

✓ Keep your operating system and apps up to date.
// recommended behavior

What you do (and shouldn't)

✕ Avoid
  • Accessing your online banking or making online payments
  • Logging into your work email or sensitive work tools
  • Entering important passwords (especially without the HTTPS padlock)
  • Accessing confidential documents
  • Letting your device connect automatically to known networks
✓ No major risk
  • Browsing HTTPS sites to read the news or do research
  • Watching streaming videos (Netflix, YouTube…)
  • Using apps that encrypt data end to end (WhatsApp, Signal…)
  • Reading news sites without logging into an account
// maximum protection

The best protection: the VPN

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and a remote server: even if someone intercepts your data, they only see an unreadable stream.

"

Using a VPN on public Wi-Fi is like whispering in a crowded room: even if someone is listening, they won't understand a thing.

Reliable & affordable VPNs
ProtonVPN Free tier

Free version available, very privacy-friendly. Ideal to get started.

Mainstream, easy to use, available on all devices.

⚠

Beware of little-known free VPNs: some resell your data or are spying tools themselves.

// security checklist

Other simple good practices

01

Turn off Wi-Fi when you're not using it

This prevents automatic connections to unknown networks.

02

Use your mobile data (4G/5G)

For sensitive operations, it's far more secure than public Wi-Fi.

03

Enable your device's firewall

And keep your system up to date: it's your first line of defense.

04

Turn off file sharing

And remote access when you're on a public network.

⚠
// special case

Working remotely from a café

If you work remotely from a café or a coworking space, the risks get higher: your company's data travels over the network. In that case, using a VPN provided by your employer is strongly recommended, even mandatory in many companies.
